1 Introduction
Campus life frequently involves small, recurring logistical tasks – picking up food from the
canteen, delivering documents between departments, forwarding library books, or getting items
across a large campus – that are inconvenient for a single student to interrupt their schedule for,
yet too minor to justify a formal courier service. BringIT is a peer-to-peer (P2P) delivery re-
quest platform designed specifically for a university campus environment. It connects students
who need something delivered (requesters) with fellow students who are willing to carry it
out along their existing route (deliverers), turning everyday campus movement into a mutually
beneficial, community-driven delivery network.
The platform is built as a full-stack web application using React for the frontend and Su-
pabase (PostgreSQL, Authentication, Realtime, and Storage) for the backend, allowing users
to authenticate securely, create and track delivery requests, communicate in real time, and build
reputation through a gamified leaderboard system.
2 Background Study
Commercial delivery and courier platforms such as Pathao, Uber, and Foodpanda solve general-
purpose delivery problems but are not tailored to the scale, cost sensitivity, or trust structure of
a university campus. Existing solutions have several shortcomings when applied to a campus
context:
• Cost inefficiency: Commercial services charge delivery fees disproportionate to the short
distances typical of a campus (e.g., one building to another).
• Lack of trust and community: Deliveries are handled by unknown third-party riders
rather than verified peers from the same institution.
• No academic-community integration: General platforms have no concept of student
identity, class schedules, or campus geography, and therefore cannot optimize for "on-
the-way" deliveries.
• Manual, informal coordination: In practice, students currently coordinate small favors
through scattered WhatsApp/Facebook groups or word of mouth, with no tracking, ac-
countability, or incentive structure.
This gap motivates the study of a purpose-built, campus-scoped delivery coordination sys-
tem that leverages student identity verification, real-time communication, and lightweight gam-
ification to build a reliable peer delivery network.
3 Motivation
The motivation for BringIT stems from recurring, first-hand observations of campus life:
1. Time scarcity: Students often cannot leave a lab, class, or hall to pick up a parcel, food
order, or document, even though another student is already walking that exact route.
2. Underutilized peer trust: Students within the same institution already share an implicit
layer of trust (verified by student ID) that commercial platforms cannot replicate.
2
3. No incentive for informal help: Existing informal delivery favors are unrewarded and
unstructured, discouraging repeated participation.
4. Opportunity for a lightweight, low-cost solution: A web application requiring no pro-
prietary hardware, built entirely on modern, free-tier-friendly technologies (React, Su-
pabase), can be deployed and iterated on quickly for a student-sized user base.
These observations directly motivate a system that formalizes peer delivery requests, adds
accountability through tracking and authentication, and rewards active deliverers – ultimately
increasing convenience for requesters and creating a rewarding, community-oriented side ac-
tivity for deliverers.
4 Application
BringIT is intended for deployment within a single university or a cluster of geographically
close campuses. Concrete use cases include:
• A student stuck in a lab session requests that a canteen order be picked up and dropped
at their department.
• A student delivers a lab report or physical document from one faculty building to another
on their way to class.
• A resident of a dormitory requests that a parcel be collected from the campus post office
/ gate.
• Frequent deliverers build a public reputation via the leaderboard, which can later be tied
to campus recognition or micro-incentive programs run by student bodies.
Beyond individual convenience, the platform has secondary value as a community-engagement
tool: it encourages students to interact across departments and years, and generates a dataset
of campus mobility and demand patterns that could inform future improvements such as route
optimization or partnership with campus administration.
5 Proposed Methodology
Figure 1 outlines the six-stage methodology used to design and build BringIT:
1. Requirement Gathering & Problem Analysis: Campus delivery pain points, user roles
(requester/deliverer), and core functional needs were identified before any design work
began.
2. System Design: The overall architecture, database schema, and UI/UX flow were planned,
laying the foundation for both frontend and backend work.
3. Parallel Development: Once the design was finalized, work split into two concurrent
tracks – Frontend Development (React + Tailwind CSS pages, components, and contexts)
and the Backend & Data Layer (Supabase authentication, PostgreSQL, Realtime, and
Storage) – allowing both halves of the stack to progress independently against the shared
design.

8 Conclusion
BringIT addresses a practical, everyday problem faced by university students by introducing a
structured, trust-based, peer-to-peer delivery platform tailored to campus life. By combining a
modern React frontend with the Supabase backend-as-a-service ecosystem, the project achieves
rapid development velocity while still delivering core requirements: secure authentication, real-
time request tracking, in-app communication, and gamified incentives. The proposed Agile
methodology and modular architecture ensure the system can be extended incrementally – fu-
ture iterations may include route-based matching, push notifications, and analytics on campus
delivery patterns. Overall, BringIT is expected to demonstrate how lightweight, community-
driven software can meaningfully improve convenience and collaboration within a university
ecosystem

1 Introduction / Background
CUET’s campus is located roughly 30 kilometers from Chittagong city, and a large share
of the student body lives on-campus in residential halls. Everyday necessities that are not
available near campus — stationery, medicine, specific food items, documents, or exam
materials — require a trip into the city that many students cannot always make on short
notice. At the same time, a significant number of students travel between the campus and
the city daily for classes, errands, or personal reasons, and this travel capacity is almost
entirely unused for anything beyond the traveler’s own purpose. Commercial courier
services such as Pathao or Sundarban exist, but their pricing and turnaround time are
not designed for small, urgent, single-item deliveries between two known points inside
a closed university community. BringIt addresses this gap by turning ordinary student
travel into a trusted, low-cost peer delivery network restricted to verified CUET students.
2 Project Objectives
Main Objective
To design and develop an AI-assisted, web-based peer-to-peer delivery platform for the
CUET campus community that connects students who need items from the city with
students who are already traveling, making delivery faster, cheaper, and more trustworthy
than existing alternatives.
Specific Objectives
•To build a request–traveler matching system that intelligently pairs delivery requests
with travelers based on route, timing, and reliability, instead of a simple open feed.
•To introduce a fair, transparent reward suggestion so requesters do not need to guess
a reasonable delivery fee.
•To establish a university-verified trust and reputation system that makes peer delivery
safe for both requesters and travelers.
•To provide real-time status tracking of requests from posting to delivery, accessible on
both desktop and mobile.
•To reduce dependency on commercial courier services for small, urgent, hyper-local
deliveries within the CUET campus and Chittagong city.
3 Scope of the Project
In Scope
•Student authentication and verification using university ID/email.
•Posting, browsing, filtering, and accepting delivery requests.
•Rule-based matching of requests to suitable travelers.

•Rule-based reward/price suggestion at the time a request is posted.
•Delivery status tracking (Open → Accepted → In Progress → Delivered).
•Star-based rating and delivery history for trust scoring.
•Responsive web dashboard usable on mobile browsers.
Out of Scope
•Native mobile applications (iOS/Android) — the platform will be a responsive web
app only in this phase.
•In-app payment processing — reward settlement will remain an off-platform arrange-
ment (e.g., cash or mobile financial services) between requester and traveler.
•Delivery outside the CUET campus–Chittagong city corridor.
4 Proposed Features
•Secure Authentication: Registration and login restricted to verified CUET student
IDs or university email addresses.
•User Profiles: Department- and hall-based profile information, delivery history, and
credibility score.
•Request Management: Posting requests with pickup/drop-off locations, item de-
scription, urgency tagging (High/Medium/Low), and search/filtering by route.
•Delivery Workflow: Job acceptance by travelers and status tracking through Open,
Accepted, In Progress, and Delivered states.
•Trust & Reputation System: Star-based ratings and a running credibility score
built from delivery history.
•Responsive Dashboard: A mobile-friendly interface for real-time updates on active
and past deliveries.
AI Integration :
•Smart Traveler–Request Matching (Rule-Based Scoring): Each open request
is scored per traveler using a weighted formula:
Score = w1·RouteOverlap+w2·TimeWindowOverlap+w3·UrgencyWeight+w4·TravelerRating
where RouteOverlap measures how closely the pickup/drop-off points sit along the
traveler’s stated path, TimeWindowOverlap measures how well the request’s timing fits
the traveler’s travel window, UrgencyWeight comes from the request’s High/Medium/Low
tag, and TravelerRating is the traveler’s own credibility score. Requests are then
ranked highest-score-first on that traveler’s feed. This is a deterministic formula, not
a trained model, so it works correctly from day one with no historical data.
